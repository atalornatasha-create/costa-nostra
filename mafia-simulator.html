The Rules Of The House

Three kinds of people are dealt into this game: one Mafia, one Nurse, and everybody else is a civilian. Nobody picks. Nobody is told who anyone is. As far as the room is concerned, every single person is just a civilian.

  1. Four people minimum. Any game needs at least four at the table. Bigger tables simply mean more civilians — there is still only ever one Mafia and one Nurse.
  2. The deal is random and secret. When the game starts you are handed Mafia, Nurse or civilian by chance. Only you see your own card.
  3. Nobody knows anybody. The Mafia does not know who the Nurse is. The Nurse does not know who the Mafia is. Civilians know nothing at all. Everyone at the table looks like a civilian.
  4. At night the Mafia kills one person. Anyone at the table — a civilian, or the Nurse.
  5. At night the Nurse saves one person. The Nurse is never told who was attacked, so the save is a guess. Guess right and that person walks away from it. Guess wrong and the person the Mafia chose is dead — nothing can undo it.
  6. The Nurse can save herself. That is the whole problem: the night she spends protecting her own skin is a night nobody else is covered, and the night she covers someone else may be the night the Mafia comes for her.
  7. By day everyone meets in the community centre. Everybody talks — and that includes the Mafia and the Nurse, sitting there pretending to be as lost as everyone else.
  8. Then the room votes. Whoever gets the most hands is dragged out and hanged, guilty or not. A tie is argued out at the table.
  9. How it ends. Civilians win the moment the Mafia is hanged. The Mafia wins when there is nobody left to stop them.
  10. The administrators. Two admins hold the sealed records and can see who everyone really is, at any time, without anyone at the table being told. Inside the game they appear only as Admin 1 and Admin 2 — their own names are never shown.
